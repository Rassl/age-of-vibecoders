About

Free 3D games that run in the browser and, on Apple Vision Pro, around you. Made by one developer, open source.

What this is

Age of Vibecoders is a small collection of games that need nothing but a web page. There is no install, no account, no payment and no advertising. Each game is a static site built with three.js and vanilla JavaScript, so it loads in a few seconds and runs on a phone, a laptop or a headset.

Apple Vision Pro

Every game here exposes an immersive WebXR session. Open the page in Safari on visionOS, choose Immersive, and the game leaves the window: your head is the camera, and you act by looking at something and pinching. Nothing is downloaded from the App Store.

  • Squad Runner — you stand on the road behind your squad; a pinch-drag moves the whole crowd across the lanes.
  • Ascent — you start in a loft. Look up and the ceiling opens; you lift off over real satellite imagery of a city of your choice (or your own location), climb past the ISS altitude and park in geostationary orbit, with landmarks labelled along the way.

If Safari reports that immersive VR is not supported, enable WebXR Device API under Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Feature Flags.

The games

Squad Runner
An action lane-runner shooter. Drag to steer; the squad fires by itself. Shoot the orbs to grow the crowd, break the barrels, earn your wings and take down the boss. Runs last about two minutes. Play
Ascent
A flight from a quiet loft, through your city, to orbit. Imagery from Esri and Maxar, buildings from OpenStreetMap, landmarks from Wikipedia and Wikidata, sky and Earth textures from Poly Haven and NASA. Play

Technology

three.js on WebGL, WebXR for the immersive mode, ES modules bundled with Vite, hosted as static files. Squad Runner generates its world and most of its assets procedurally at boot; the enemy and drone models are CC BY works from Sketchfab, credited in the repository README. Both games are in one public repository: github.com/Rassl/age-of-vibecoders.
